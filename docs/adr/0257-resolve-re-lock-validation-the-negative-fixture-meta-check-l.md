## Why

validation owns the validator's law and execution model, so the meta-check law, the `custom/script` contract, and the mechanical-green operator disclosure land here first, before the grammar ([check](../spec/systems/surfaces/check.md), D-258) and the instance ([validators-core](../spec/modules/validators-core.md), D-259). The four-lens gate earned its keep: it closed the one real over-trust hole (the open-category carve-out) and two seam defects (the half-authored fixture grammar, the over-asserted dispatcher affordance) before they could propagate into the later carriers as facts a locked doc had already fixed. The fixes keep each downstream hand-off single-owned and the altitude honest.

## What we ruled out

**Lock with the open-category carve-out** (rejected — it lets a future green no-op self-classify as "environment-conditionally vacuous" and dodge the gate; bounded to the statically-decidable-failure-path property, the [D-219](0219-authorize-the-first-run-travel-safety-re-litigation-a-retire.md) define-by-property discipline). **Author the fixture-namespace grammar here** (rejected — it is the [check](../spec/systems/surfaces/check.md)/[repository-topology](../spec/systems/infrastructure/repository-topology.md) master's; validation keeps only its execution-model stake, the "one master" propagation rule). **Assert the dispatcher entry point as existing** (rejected — its existence-vs-seam-bullet is the D-260 adjudication; stated as core's-to-provide). **Hold the validation re-lock until D-258/D-260 land** (rejected — the dependency order is contract/law first; the forward references are the accepted [D-220](0220-resolve-the-d-219-first-run-travel-safety-re-litigation-land.md) authorize-then-resolve transient, closed in the same pass).
