# Q25 re-litigation: a fourth v1-core policy — the Triage-threshold policy (telemetry's promotion thresholds)

*Decided 2026-05-29 in the design workspace.*

## The decision

Resolve Q25 (a `core`-lock blocker, [D-110](0110-run-the-genesis-dry-run-through-complete-core-log-the-build.md)) by adding a **fourth v1-core policy**, the **Triage-threshold policy**, to the locked [policies](../spec/systems/surfaces/policies.md) surface and [core](../spec/modules/core.md)'s `provides`. It holds [telemetry](../spec/systems/guardrails/telemetry.md)'s promotion thresholds — the **persistence threshold** (benign-signal promotion latency), the **auto-resolve** observation count, and the **triage-pressure** threshold — in a governed, legible policy that telemetry *reads*, rather than buried as code constants. It is framed honestly as foundational to **telemetry's operation**, **not a trust-model peer** of the original three (contract-threshold, finding-disposition, escalation); its enforcement tier is **posture** (the mechanical reading is telemetry's; this policy's force is the legibility expectation). Backstage vocabulary (streams, severity class, persistence threshold) stays in the policy and telemetry's code and never reaches the operator; the policy's Rationale is plain-language. This is the **litigation-alarm re-litigation** of the locked policies doc ([D-035](0035-re-litigation-policies-retargets-the-contract-threshold-narr.md)), approved by the operator this session, and the first locked-doc step of the core-lock closure wave ([D-112](0112-author-the-core-lock-closure-procedure-the-q24-q27-remediati.md)/[D-113](0113-core-lock-closure-phase-0-the-build-spec-leaf-form-contract.md)). **Propagation** (the change-propagation matrix): the policies doc gains the four-policy intro + a `### Triage-threshold` section; [core](../spec/modules/core.md) `provides` lists `triage-threshold`; [glossary](../reference/glossary.md)'s `Policy` term enumerates four and its existing `Triage-threshold policy` term is cross-referenced as the fourth; [migration-discipline](../spec/modules/migration-discipline.md)'s "one of the three v1-core policies" is reconciled to count-agnostic "one of the v1-core policies"; Q25 is deleted from [open-questions.md](../reference/open-questions.md). No `risks.md` reconciliation is owed — [R4](../reference/risks.md) is the *attention* hardcoded-constants risk and Q25 changes no risk status (the threshold-in-policy design pre-existed; Q25 only homes the policy in the v1-core set), so the policies section drops the mismatched R4 citation rather than perpetuating it.

## Why

[D-110](0110-run-the-genesis-dry-run-through-complete-core-log-the-build.md)'s genesis dry-run found the telemetry promotion/auto-resolve/triage-pressure machinery `core` must ship had **nowhere to read its thresholds**: telemetry's locked doc and the glossary both already presuppose a "governed promotion policy instance" (the Triage-threshold policy), but the policies doc's v1-core set was fixed at exactly three and no module's `provides` shipped it — the *policy instance itself* was unowned (missing grammar, unlike attention whose instance `core` already ships). Adding the fourth v1-core policy is the justified re-litigation the lock discipline sanctions (the slot genuinely belongs in the v1-core set; the lock stops silent drift, not justified edits), chosen over a sibling owner (rejected below). The fourth-as-operational framing answers the cold-audit finding that a fourth *trust-model* policy would falsify "foundational to the trust model" for the original three. A **focused cold re-audit** of the as-edited doc ran before this re-lock (the [D-104](0104-phase-c-cross-reference-the-external-contribution-mode-into.md) precedent): two independent lenses caught a **blocking** invented enforcement tier ("configuration/posture", outside the closed `hard-fail`/`soft-warn`/`posture` set) and a **serious** coined threshold label ("benign-promotion persistence" vs telemetry's "persistence threshold"); both were reworked to the legal tier (**posture**, the dependency-/migration-discipline precedent) and telemetry's exact term, the mismatched R4 citation and an unsupported "consecutive" qualifier were dropped, and a confirmation re-audit on the reworked doc verified it clean. The wider four-lens **plan** audit (run before this wave began) had already cleared the Q25 decision and surfaced the glossary/migration-discipline propagation targets folded above. `python3 lock.py --relock systems/surfaces/policies/README.md --decision D-114`; `python3 validate.py` green.

## What we ruled out

Name a non-`core` **sibling owner** (rejected — telemetry rides `core`, so the policy rides `core` regardless, and there is no natural sibling; the v1-core set is the home telemetry/glossary already presuppose). Fold the thresholds into an **existing** policy (rejected — the three trust-model policies govern disposition/escalation, the wrong home for telemetry tuning knobs; a dedicated policy matches the glossary term and telemetry's "governed policy instance" reference). Call it a **fourth trust-model policy** / recount "Three are foundational to the trust model" to "Four" (rejected on audit — it is operational tuning, not a trust-model peer; the honest framing keeps the three as the trust-model set and adds the fourth as operational). Declare its tier **"configuration"** or soft-warn/hard (rejected — "configuration" is outside the closed enforcement-tier set; the values are read by telemetry's machinery, so the policy's own force is **posture**). Keep the **R4** citation for consistency with telemetry (rejected — R4's text is attention-scoped; citing it for a telemetry threshold misdirects a reader, and Q25 changes no risk status). Skip the re-audit / hand-compute the fingerprint (rejected — `lock.py` owns the fingerprint, and the as-locked artifact gets a cold re-audit before the lock stands).
