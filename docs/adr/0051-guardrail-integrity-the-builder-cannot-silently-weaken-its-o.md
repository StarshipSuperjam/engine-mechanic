# Guardrail integrity: the builder cannot silently weaken its own enforcement (principle §15)

*Decided 2026-05-25 in the design workspace.*

## The decision

Resolve [Q13](../reference/open-questions.md) — the highest-severity trust gap in the corpus — **additively**, as a new **[principle §15](../principles.md)** plus a recorded mechanism for the implementation phase, with **no edit to any locked doc** (no litigation alarm). The law: the in-session builder may *change* the Engine's own enforcement config (`.claude/settings.json` deny/permission blocks, `.github/workflows/`, validation/check definitions, `CODEOWNERS`, the branch ruleset) — a module install legitimately *strengthens* it — but may never *weaken* it *silently*. A **weakening-class** diff (remove / disable / rename / loosen a guardrail, or edit the ruleset-affecting files) is a governance-critical event: it **hard-blocks the merge until an explicit operator acknowledgment is present**, and is surfaced in plain language (*which* protection weakened and what the AI could then do unwatched, never a bare "config changed"); a **strengthening-class** diff passes without alarm. The mechanism recorded for the build phase (a leaf, not built now): a required CI **check** (the locked `custom/script` kind, an additive instance) that **classifies the diff** and hard-blocks weakening pending acknowledgment; the check is **bound in the branch ruleset by its frozen name** ([D-048](0048-provisioning-delivery-designed-end-state-brownfield-capable.md)) so deleting/renaming it deadlocks the merge ("expected check never reported"), and is **evaluated from a trusted source** (`pull_request_target`-class, reading the diff only, never executing head code) so the guarding PR cannot disarm its own guard; plain-language surfacing rides the locked [control-plane](../spec/systems/infrastructure/control-plane.md) PR Risk + Claude-involvement sections and [boot](../spec/systems/lifecycle/boot.md); [build-orchestration](../spec/systems/lifecycle/build-orchestration.md)'s plan-gate risk assessment names enforcement-config weakening as a headline risk trigger. Closure is **tiered honestly**: **solo** = *cannot weaken silently* (blocked until informed consent — the merge gate is the wall); **team** = *cannot weaken at all* (a distinct identity alone holds `admin:repo_ruleset`). Provisioning is flagged that lowering team-identity setup friction is the path to a structural option for the solo operator. Resolves Q13 and reconciles R1's settings-tampering pointer.

## Why

The operator's whole bargain is "the checks are real, so I can walk away," yet nothing prevented the builder from disarming those checks — and the builder lives inside the very repo whose enforcement protects it. The cold-session design audit ([D-018](0018-cold-session-design-audit-required-before-any-lock.md)) ran four lenses (adversarial / technical-feasibility / non-engineer-operator / architect) against the resolution; their blocking/serious findings were resolved before this entry, materially reshaping it: **(1)** the *self-guarding-check bypass* — GitHub runs the PR-branch's workflow for `pull_request`, so a PR can disarm the check meant to catch it — is closed by ruleset-binding the frozen check name (deletion → merge deadlock, verified) and trusted-source evaluation; **(2)** *operator wording + alarm fatigue* — "config changed" is not informed consent and firing on benign installs trains rubber-stamping — is closed by the weakening-vs-strengthening discrimination and the plain-language "what protection weakened" surfacing; **(3)** the *tier contradiction* (`tier:hard` cannot mean "doesn't prevent") is closed by hard-block-until-acknowledged, where the human acknowledgment is the pass condition (§6); **(4)** the *hook-ownership orphan* — an always-on integrity gate is orthogonal to stance, so it is not [modes](../spec/systems/lifecycle/modes.md)' concern (its explore write-gate is stance-scoped and would *permit* the write in Build/Routine), and its natural owner [validation](../spec/systems/guardrails/validation.md) is locked — combined with the gate's fallibility (the platform ignores `PreToolUse` deny for some Edit/Write paths, verified) and its non-operator-facing nature, justified **dropping the standalone `PreToolUse` member** entirely; in-session visibility rides build-orchestration's existing risk assembly. The additive/no-re-lock claim was architect-confirmed part-by-part (control-plane locks that the ruleset requires checks, not which names; hooks' block budget is irrelevant once the member is dropped; check instances are additive data; trusted-source evaluation is a harness trigger detail). Honest tiering follows [R3](../reference/risks.md) (no over-claim): solo is mitigated, not airtight, and the docs say so.

## What we ruled out

State the invariant as law inside locked control-plane/hooks (rejected by the operator — the additive principle achieves the guarantee without a re-litigation alarm). Keep the standalone always-on `PreToolUse` block member (rejected — orphaned ownership since its natural owner is locked, fallible per the platform deny-handling gap, and not operator-facing; it added no load-bearing guarantee the merge-gate check does not). A check that simply *surfaces* every enforcement-config diff (rejected — alarm fatigue destroys the signal exactly when a weakening change must stand out; discriminate weakening from strengthening). `tier:hard` that "does not prevent, only acknowledges" (rejected — contradicts the locked check semantics; the hard block's pass condition is the explicit human acknowledgment). Evaluate the check under plain `pull_request` (rejected — the guarding PR could neuter its own guard; trusted-source evaluation is required). Claim solo mode structurally prevents tampering (rejected — over-claim; solo blocks *silent* weakening and team alone prevents weakening outright). Mint §15 only after building the check (rejected — the law precedes the leaf per [D-003](0003-specify-the-full-end-state-before-the-first-build-pr.md); the mechanism is recorded, not yet built).
